# ADR-0003 — Manually seed theatre data; do not scrape

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-02-09
- **Deciders:** Founder
- **Milestone:** [M2](../../product/milestones/M2.md)
- **Supersedes:** none

---

## Context

GoldSeats recommends the best available seat in a specific auditorium for a specific
showtime. That requires three kinds of theatre data:

1. **Auditorium geometry** — rows, seats per row, aisle positions, row curvature, stadium
   elevation, screen width and height, distance from the front row to the screen. The
   scoring engine's view-angle and vertical-position factors are direct functions of these
   numbers.
2. **Showtimes** — which film, which auditorium, when, in what format and language.
3. **Seat availability** — which seats are already taken, which drives the neighbour
   openness factor and the entire notion of a "best *available* seat".

None of this is published as an API by Canadian theatre chains. Cineplex has no public
developer programme. The obvious engineering answer is to scrape their seat picker — the
data is right there in the page, one HTTP request away, and the technique is well
understood.

The constraints that make that the wrong answer:

- **Terms of service.** Cineplex's terms, like effectively every ticketing site's, prohibit
  automated access and data extraction. Scraping would put us in breach from the first
  request.
- **We need a commercial relationship with these companies.** Our entire monetisation
  hypothesis is a deep-link handoff into the theatre's own booking flow, which means
  affiliate or partnership revenue. Starting that relationship by violating their ToS is
  self-defeating in a way that's hard to recover from. "We were scraping you but we'd like
  to talk about a partnership" is not a conversation that goes well.
- **Legal exposure in Canada.** Beyond contract, there's the *Copyright Act* on database
  compilations and PIPEDA on anything incidental. A pre-revenue company with one engineer
  should not be acquiring legal risk to save seeding effort.
- **Scrapers are operationally expensive.** They break on every markup change, need
  anti-bot circumvention, and produce silent data corruption when a selector shifts
  meaning instead of disappearing. That's a permanent maintenance burden on a one-person
  team.
- **Scraping wouldn't actually give us the geometry we need.** A seat picker renders a
  grid of seat IDs and availability states. It does not publish screen width in metres,
  first-row distance, stadium rise, or row curvature. Those are the *inputs to our scoring
  model*, and they'd have to be inferred or hand-measured regardless.

And the asset that changes the calculation entirely: `goldseats-dataset/generator/theatre.py`
already generates realistic auditorium geometry for five theatre types — standard, large,
small, imax, recliners — with rows, aisles, curvature, stadium elevation, and an IMAX
premium centre section. The scoring model was tuned against layouts produced by that code.

## Decision

**We do not scrape. Theatre data is hand-authored, committed to git, and loaded by a
seeding CLI.**

Concretely, for [M2](../../product/milestones/M2.md):

- **5 to 10 real auditoriums**, hand-authored from public floor plans, published seating
  charts, venue specification pages, and in-person observation.
- Each layout is a versioned JSON document committed at
  `seeds/layouts/<theatre-slug>-<auditorium>.v<N>.json`, conforming to a versioned
  `seat_layout` schema.
- **Layouts reuse the geometry logic from `generator/theatre.py`.** A seeded auditorium is
  the real row-and-seat arrangement, with its geometry produced by the same code that
  generated the training layouts. This matters: if seeded geometry used a different
  coordinate convention or a different stadium-rise model than the layouts the weights were
  tuned against, the scores would be subtly and invisibly wrong.
- `app/cli/seed_theatre.py` loads these files idempotently, keyed on a natural key, and
  projects `layout_json` into queryable `seats` rows.
- Every layout records `seeded_by`, `seeded_at`, `source_file`, and a `notes` field saying
  where the measurements came from and when they were verified.
- **Showtimes are hand-seeded** for the seeded auditoriums, each with a `booking_links` row
  carrying a validated outbound URL.
- **Seat availability**, until a partner data path exists, is generated by the booking
  simulator from `generator/seats.py` — centre-first, back rows before middle, aisle
  preference, front-row penalty, 1–4 seat group chunks clustered into natural islands.
  Every such snapshot is stored with `source = 'simulated'` and `confidence = 'low'`, and
  the UI says so.
- `seat_availability_snapshots.source` has a `CHECK` constraint permitting `manual`,
  `simulated`, and `partner_api`. **There is no `scraped` value.** The policy is expressed
  as a database constraint, so violating it requires a migration, a PR, and someone
  explaining themselves.

The policy is written for contributors in
[`../../legal/data-sources-policy.md`](../../legal/data-sources-policy.md). A pull request
adding a scraper is closed, not reviewed.

### How we expand

In priority order:

1. **Partnerships.** Approach Cineplex and others for an affiliate or data relationship. Our
   pitch — we send you pre-qualified buyers and take no payment data — is genuinely
   attractive and only works if we're clean.
2. **Manual expansion.** Seeding one auditorium is a few hours. Twenty auditoriums covers a
   meaningful share of Toronto moviegoing.
3. **Licensed aggregators.** Paid showtime data feeds exist. When revenue exists, that's a
   purchase.
4. **User contributions.** Long-term: let users submit corrections to a layout, reviewed
   before merge. A crowdsourced seating chart is a legitimate asset.

## Options considered

### Option A — Hand-seed, reusing the generator's geometry (chosen)

- **Pros:** Zero legal risk. Zero ToS violation. Preserves the partnership path. Geometry is
  consistent with what the model was tuned on. Data is version-controlled, reviewable, and
  diffable — a change to a seating chart shows up in a PR. No scraper maintenance. Forces us
  to be excellent in a small area before being mediocre across the country.
- **Cons:** Doesn't scale without labour. Coverage is our biggest product limitation. Manual
  data goes stale silently when a theatre reconfigures. Showtimes genuinely change weekly,
  making hand-seeded showtimes the weakest part of this plan.

### Option B — Scrape theatre sites

- **Pros:** Broad coverage immediately. Real availability data. No manual labour.
- **Cons:** ToS violation from request one. Poisons the partnership relationship that is our
  monetisation path. Legal exposure. Permanent scraper maintenance. Anti-bot arms race.
  Silent data corruption. **And it still wouldn't give us screen dimensions or row
  geometry** — the inputs the scoring model actually needs.
- **Why not:** It's the wrong trade on every axis simultaneously. Even ignoring law and
  ethics entirely, it doesn't solve the hard part of the problem.

### Option C — Fully synthetic layouts only

- **Pros:** Infinite scale, zero legal exposure, already built. The generator produces
  realistic auditoriums today.
- **Cons:** **The recommendation would be fiction.** Telling a user that Row H seat 12 is the
  golden seat at Scotiabank Theatre when we've never looked at Scotiabank Theatre's floor
  plan is a lie dressed as a product. It's also unfalsifiable-until-they-arrive, which is the
  worst kind of wrong.
- **Why not:** Synthetic data is for training and testing. Shipping it as a recommendation
  destroys the only thing we have, which is being right about seats.

### Option D — Ask users to enter the layout themselves

- **Pros:** Scales with users, no legal risk.
- **Cons:** Enormous friction before any value is delivered. Nobody counts rows in a cinema
  lobby to use an app they've never tried. Quality would be unusable without review, and
  review is the labour we were trying to avoid.
- **Why not:** Wrong side of the cold-start problem. Viable later as *corrections* to
  existing layouts, which is a much smaller ask.

### Option E — Buy a licensed showtime data feed now

- **Pros:** Legitimate, broad, maintained.
- **Cons:** Costs money we don't have. Feeds carry showtimes and formats, **not auditorium
  geometry** — so it solves our second-hardest problem and none of our hardest one.
- **Why not:** No budget, and incomplete for our needs. Revisit with revenue, as a
  supplement rather than a replacement.

## Consequences

### What this makes easier

- Legal and partnership posture is clean. We can approach any chain without a conversation
  about past behaviour.
- Theatre data is code: reviewed, versioned, diffable. A change to a seating chart goes
  through a PR like anything else.
- No scraper infrastructure, no proxies, no anti-bot maintenance, no 3am scraper-broke
  alerts.
- Data quality is high for what we cover, because a human looked at it.
- Geometry consistency with the training layouts means the scores mean what we think they
  mean.
- Forces focus. Being genuinely excellent for ten Toronto auditoriums is a better product
  than being wrong about a thousand.

### What this makes harder

- **Coverage is the product's headline limitation**, and it's the first thing users will
  notice. The UI has to handle "we don't have this theatre yet" gracefully and honestly, and
  should capture demand for unseeded theatres.
- **Showtime freshness is the weakest link.** Showtimes change weekly; hand-seeding them is
  sustainable for ten auditoriums and not for a hundred. This is the constraint most likely
  to force this ADR's revision.
- **Availability is simulated**, so the neighbour-openness factor is operating on plausible
  fiction. We must be honest about it in the UI, and it caps how confident we can be.
- Staleness is silent. A theatre converting to recliners invalidates a layout with no signal
  to us. Mitigated by `seat_layouts` versioning, `last_verified_at` on booking links, and a
  periodic re-verification pass.

### What this commits us to

- Building the seeding CLI and the versioned `seat_layout` schema properly in
  [M2](../../product/milestones/M2.md) — this is production data tooling, not a script.
- Being honest in the UI about availability confidence. Non-negotiable.
- Keeping the `seat_availability_snapshots.source` constraint free of a `scraped` value.
- Respecting `robots.txt` and ToS for every source we ever add, including news feeds in
  [M8](../../product/milestones/M8.md).
- Actively pursuing partnerships as the real answer to coverage, rather than treating manual
  seeding as a permanent strategy.

## Revisit when

- **A chain grants us API access or an affiliate data feed.** That's a new ADR describing the
  ingestion path, and it's the outcome we're playing for.
- **Showtime maintenance exceeds roughly two hours a week.** That's the signal that manual
  showtime seeding has hit its ceiling, and it will happen well before layout seeding does.
- **A licensed aggregator becomes affordable.** Supplement, not replacement — it still won't
  carry geometry.
- **Coverage becomes the top reason users churn**, measured rather than assumed. Then the
  answer is more seeding labour or a partnership, never a scraper.

Note what is *not* a revisit trigger: finding a scraper that's technically easy, or observing
that a competitor scrapes. Neither changes the analysis.
